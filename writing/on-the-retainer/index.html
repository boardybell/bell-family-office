On the Fee

Why the firm charges an annual fee as a percentage of assets, and what that structure reveals about the work.

8 January 2026

Almost every firm in this category charges a percentage of assets under management. It has been the industry standard for decades. It is the structure we use, and the reason is worth explaining.

The fee is a percentage of assets, set also by the complexity of the engagement. A straightforward situation pays a lower rate. A situation involving multiple trusts, a private operating business, second-generation heirs, significant philanthropy, or active succession questions pays a higher one. The portfolio measures the scope of what is being stewarded. The complexity measures the work. The fee reflects both.

This structure removes the most common conflict in the category, which is compensation that flows from selling something. The firm sells no products. It takes no commission, no revenue share, and no payment from any outside manager. Its only income is the fee the client pays it directly. There is nothing else pulling on a recommendation.

The fee is charged on assets, but the assets are not where most of the consequential decisions get made. The drafting of instruments, the coordination of counsel, the navigation of transitions, the preparation of heirs, these are the activities that shape a family's situation over decades. They are included in the fee. A client is not billed separately for the hours that matter most.

The percentage fee ties the firm's compensation to the whole of a family's capital and to the long work of keeping it intact. Where a fee charged on assets creates its own pull, toward holding what might be better distributed, the firm answers it plainly. It recommends distribution when distribution is correct, and it says so in writing, even when the recommendation reduces the fee it is paid.
